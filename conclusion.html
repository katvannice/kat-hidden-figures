Hidden Figures — Technocultural Analysis

The Record, Set Straight

Hidden Figures Hidden Figures revealed several ways that race and gender determined not just who received credit, but what technological futures were made possible, and who was allowed to build them. Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan, and Mary Jackson were essential to one of humanity’s biggest and most defining achievements, yet history almost swallowed them whole.

Johnson's bathroom scene showed a direct example of technocultural entanglement. Stafford's dismissal showed erasure. Vaughan's FORTRAN arc showed exactly who really gets to build the future, and who builds it anyway, whether the institution notices or not.

The hidden figures problem did not end when the credits rolled.

The people building today’s technology such as artificial intelligence, algorithms, and computing systems, are still working within cultural barriers that determine whose contributions get recognized and who get quietly swept under the rug. Johnson’s, Vaughan’s, and Jackson’s names were just a few that were recovered. Not everyone’s names will be.

This is the exact reason why technoculture demands we keep asking who's missing from the story. Because the future is always being built by those quietly working behind the scenes, and whose spotlight hasn't been found yet.
