Hidden Figures — Technocultural Analysis
Concept III

Future and Its History

"The future is imaginary — it exists as a projection of what comes next and as a trace of how the future was imagined yesterday." Anne Balsamo — Introduction to TechnoCulture (10.1)

Dorothy Vaughan understood this better than anyone at NASA. While the institution still had no idea what to do with the IBM computer sitting dormant in the tech wing, Vaughan had already seen the writing on the wall.

She doesn’t wait for the future to arrive; she prepares for it alone before anyone asks her to.

Dorothy Vaughan — mathematician, supervisor, and the woman who got the IBM running when no one else could.

The Library

Vaughan goes to the library to find a book on the FORTRAN programming language. But even accessing knowledge about technology was racially gatekept. She is told the colored section doesn't have what she needs (Hidden Figures, 00:49:53). She is escorted out regardless, the FORTRAN book already stuffed in her purse.

When her son questions her on the bus, Vaughan states:

"You can't take something you already paid for." Dorothy Vaughan — Hidden Figures (00:50:40)

Paying taxes, just like everyone else, should give her the right to knowledge and the future. She claims both.

The IBM Computer

The IBM 7090 Data Processing System, which was capable of over 24,000 multiplications per second.

Vaughan teaches her entire unit FORTRAN unofficially, unpaid, and unrecognized (Hidden Figures, 00:59:03). Then, when the IBM machine sits stationary and unused, and while engineers puzzle over it, Vaughan quietly tinkers and gets it running.

"Well, I'll be. What's your name again?" Bill Calhoun, IBM — Hidden Figures (1:19:42)

He didn't know her name until she made the machine run. Vaughan built the future for herself and her entire unit while the institution tried to lock her out of it.
