Setting the Record Straight

Hidden Figures (2016) takes a close look at NASA's space program to reveal the Black women mathematicians whose labor made the space race possible. These women’s names were nearly lost in time due to constant discredit and institutional erasure.

Diving into the concepts of technocultural entanglement, hidden figures, and the future and its history, the film reveals how race and gender determined not just who received credit, but what technological futures were made possible in the first place.

The Three Women at the Center

Katherine Johnson- a mathematician whose orbital trajectory calculations were so precise that astronaut John Glenn refused to fly without her personal verification.

Dorothy Vaughan- a supervisor in all but title, who taught herself the FORTRAN programming language, and prepared her entire unit for the age of computing before anyone asked her to.

Mary Jackson- NASA's first black female engineer, who had to petition a court just to attend the classes she needed to earn that title.

Their stories are the foundation of NASA's legacy.
