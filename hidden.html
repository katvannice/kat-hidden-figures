Hidden Figures — Technocultural Analysis
Concept II

Hidden Figures

Balsamo defines hidden figures as people who have made significant contributions to technological development but have been hidden from history for several reasons (Balsamo 5.6). The film puts names and faces to that definition.

Katherine Johnson — Erased from Her Own Work

Katherine Johnson at NASA Langley Research Center.

Johnson calculates John Glenn's orbital trajectory, which was one of the most significant mathematical tasks in NASA history. Yet Paul Stafford, a head engineer, continuously attempts to reduce her to simply her job title.

"Computers don't author reports." Paul Stafford — Hidden Figures (1:14:55)

In four words, Stafford weaponizes her job title to erase her authorship. Johnson is the reason the numbers work. She doesn't just calculate them. Her calculations are the backbone of a mission that would define American history, yet her name belongs to no report.

This isn’t a minor administrative oversight; it’s a purposeful cultural structure. The same structure that has kept numerous contributions buried beneath those with more institutional power.

Mary Jackson — Petitioning for the Right to Learn

Mary Jackson, NASA's first Black female engineer.

Mary Jackson's story is the hidden figures concept made literal. To become a NASA engineer, she needed to attend advanced classes. These classes were held at a segregated school, so she was legally barred from entering.

She petitioned the court for the right to attend. A judge granted it after much convincing. She became NASA's first black female engineer. But the fact that she had to argue her case before a judge just to access education tells us everything about how the system was designed, and for whom.

Even though Johnson and Jackson were constrained by social prejudices and legal sanctions, their perseverance influenced new ideas, technological practices, and social-political arrangements (Balsamo 5.6). Credit isn't always restored. But their persistence changed things anyway.
